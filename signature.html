    About SIGNATURE

    SIGNATURE · Field Instrument 127 · version 1.0.0

    A bookbinding imposition bench. A manuscript or a PDF goes in, folded signatures come out: scrambled sheets that fold into a real book, a punching template for the sewing stations, and the spine and case arithmetic that follows from the block you just made.

    How the fold is worked out

    The imposition is not a lookup table. Each format is folded in simulation with explicit geometry, and the page order, the rotations, the spine edge and the role of every crease are read back off the folded result. The instrument checks its own work: a section whose spine does not land on the left edge is refused, because it would have to be turned a quarter turn to read.

    What changed

    1.7 is a clearing up rather than a new capability. Every warning the instrument can raise now goes into one list, so a fault found at the sewing bench is visible from the station where you commit paper, counted on the station tab and listed under Before you print. A job bar under the tabs keeps the decisions you have made in view from everywhere. The unit moved to the masthead, where a setting that governs every number belongs. The fine typesetting choices, the back matter, the diagnostics and the secondary sheets are folded away, and printing is numbered in the order you do it.

    1.6 prints a bench punch list: every measurement in the order you actually work, with a box to tick and a line to write the block thickness you really got. The instrument can also be installed as an app when it is served over http, and says plainly that from a file on disk there is nothing to install because it is already offline.

    1.5 hands work to the rest of the bench without inventing a file format. The case parts leave as SVG and DXF, which the laser instruments already read, and the materials leave as a BOM CSV. A saved RSS or Atom file can be read in as a manuscript, so a year of reading becomes a commonplace book.

    1.4 adds Coptic link stitch sewn into drilled boards, longstitch through a slit wrapper, and Japanese stab sewing, which is not folded at all and switches the instrument to single leaves and a cut line. Rounding and backing became real arc geometry instead of a fudge factor, and headbands are checked against the square they have to fit in.

    1.3 reads a real PDF and imposes it. A source page is never decoded, only relocated: its content stream and every resource it reaches are copied across byte for byte inside a Form XObject, so text stays text, images are not resampled, and a rotated or cropped page arrives the way it looked.

    1.2 sets type properly. Total fit line breaking after Knuth and Plass, a conservative rule hyphenator with your own exception list, widow and orphan control, drop caps, small caps chapter openings, running heads, and half title, dedication and colophon.

    1.1 adds push out compensation, per edge trim, and collation marks: a letter at the tail of each section and a stepped bar across the spine fold that makes a mis-gathered block visible from a foot away.

    1.0.1 corrected the fold order for quarto and sextodecimo. Both had the spine landing on the head edge, which made a section that could not be read without turning it. Anything printed at those two formats before this version should be discarded.

    Why the name

    A signature is the folded sheet that makes a section of a book, and it is also the mark that says who made the thing. Both readings apply.

    Embedded assets

    No external assets. All art is inline SVG drawn by the instrument. Type metrics for the PDF writer are the standard Adobe base fourteen widths for Times, Helvetica and Courier, which are not copyrightable data and are published in the PDF specification. No fonts are embedded: the PDF names the base fourteen and the viewer supplies them.

    Known limitations

    Rounding and backing are modelled as a circular arc. Real backing depends on how the paper takes a hammer, so the arc fraction is yours to set and the shoulder is checked against the board rather than assumed.

    The DXF is AutoCAD 2000 with closed polylines and circles, which is all flat rectangular parts and round holes need. The BOM column names are a proposal, not a standard; if the quoting bench wants different headings they are one edit away in the file it reads.

    Reading a feed strips it to plain text. Images, links and formatting do not survive, because a manuscript here is text with chapter marks and nothing else.

    A side sewn book does not open flat, and no arithmetic will fix that. The gutter allowance is a judgement you make, and the instrument only warns when the sewing would run through the text.

    Hyphenation is a rule hyphenator, not Liang's pattern method, so it declines far more breaks than a typesetting system would take. It should rarely invent a wrong one. Add your own exceptions when it misses a word you use often.

    Reading a PDF needs the browser's DecompressionStream, which every current browser has. Encrypted PDFs are refused. A page whose content is split across several streams is expanded and re-joined, so those pages come out larger than they went in; the common single stream case is copied untouched. Lines that carry a drop cap or small caps are positioned word by word rather than as one run, which slightly weakens copy and paste on those few lines only.

    Character coverage is the printable ASCII range plus a small map of common punctuation. Swell, board and cloth allowances are working estimates for a bench, not shop specifications: measure the block you actually sewed.

    Push out is a geometric model. Real paper crushes at the fold, so the retained fraction is yours to set, and the honest way to set it is to fold a dummy section and measure it.
